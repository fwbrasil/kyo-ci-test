**Fork 1: removing `Arrow.apply(v: A)`**

Static analysis only. Nothing was compiled, run or measured; every claim below is read from the sources named, at the tip of the `robustness` worktree (`8cdb379d4d`).

## 1. The answer

Yes, the settled overload can be removed: its default body (`Arrow.scala:63-65`, `this.head(v, this.tail)` with the implicit lift firing on `v`, the file's one lift summon) is already the pending path with the lift folded in, so every site that reaches the default keeps its exact runtime path once the lift moves to the call site, and the only fast paths lost are the four overrides that hand `v` straight to their function (`Arrow.apply` at `Arrow.scala:168`, `recursive` at `:212`, `Ensure` at `:248` with `Bracket.scala:110`, `deferInline` at `Effect.scala:91`). It costs three things: those four direct `f(v)` calls become one `Pending` type test, one `Nested.unnest` test and, for three of the four, a safepoint enter and exit; the surviving `apply[S2]` has to default to `this.head(v, this.tail)` instead of its current `this(v, Arrow.id)` (`Arrow.scala:73-74`), or every settled application on a composed cont (`Chain`, `Arrow.scala:271-272`) allocates a `Defer` and takes an eval round; and six sites that hand a computation to a cont typed over that computation (`Batch.scala:178` and five test lines) must spell the as-data delivery as `Kyo.lift`, while `Ensure` needs a member name for its body that is not `apply`. It buys one application law, an arrow applied to a computation runs it and a computation handed over as data is nested with `Kyo.lift`, in place of a pair whose reading depends on the argument's static type and whose two readings produced the `Batch` regression (`12074d8523`) and the two `PollTest` ascriptions (`4c64b3e6fb` names the overload as the cause), and it closes the erased-`Any` settled entry through which `Eval.release` today hands an `Ensure` a union-represented value without unnesting it (`Eval.scala:645`).

Two corrections to the brief's premises, both read from the tree. First, `applyPending` does not itself test for `Pending`: its body is `this(v, Arrow.id)` (`Arrow.scala:74`), which delegates to the receiver's two-argument apply, so the `Pending` test, the deferral and the settled application belong to the receiver: a `Transform` performs them (`Arrow.scala:169-181`), and a `Chain` defers unconditionally (`Arrow.scala:271-272`). Second, there is no `Boxed` marker in this tree (the only match for the word is the test effect `AskBoxed`, `ArrowEffectTest.scala:2032`); what is nested is decided by runtime class in `Nested.nest` (`Nested.scala:30-34`) and, at compile time, by the primitive list in `Implicits.lift` (`Implicits.scala:27-36`).

## 3. The boxed payload question

What the lift emits (`Implicits.scala:27-36`): for `Int | Long | Float | Double | Boolean | Byte | Short | Char | Unit | String` at a concrete type, `v.asInstanceOf[A < S]`, a bare cast (pinned at 8 and 2 bytes by `PendingBytecodeTest.scala:34-41`); for any other type, abstract `A` included, `Nested.nest[A, S](v)` (`Nested.scala:30-34`), one `instanceof (Pending | Nested)` and a `Nested` allocation only on a hit (pinned as "one runtime test" by `PendingBytecodeTest.scala:44-51`). The lift is gated by `CanLift`, which refuses a type that is syntactically a computation (`CanLift.scala:58-59`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:100`); `Kyo.lift` (`Kyo.scala:32`) is the public spelling that takes no `CanLift` and nests exactly once (`CONTRIBUTING.md:335`).

The contract (`CONTRIBUTING.md:5`, `:49-74`): nest once at the lift boundary, carry opaquely, unnest once at delivery; a lift firing on an already union-represented value adds a layer nothing removes (`Nested.scala:13-14`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:74`); kernel files do not summon the lift (`CONTRIBUTING.md:101`); a cast in place of the lift where the payload can be a computation is a defect (`CONTRIBUTING.md:103`).

Today's settled path already lifts once and unnests once. The default `apply(v: A)` hands `v: A` to the two-argument apply's `A < S2` parameter (`Arrow.scala:65`), which is where the lift fires (`Nested.nest`, since `A` is abstract there), and the two-argument apply then unnests (`Arrow.scala:178`, `Pending.scala:80`, `ArrowEffect.scala:111`, and every other settled arm). With one `apply`, a raw argument is lifted at the call site instead (bare cast where the type is a listed primitive, `Nested.nest` otherwise) and the same two-argument apply unnests once. The four overrides skip both steps today and hand `v` to `f` directly; with one `apply` they go through lift-then-unnest, which is the identity on the payload. Verdict: no representation change and no double-nest risk at any site, provided the kernel-internal sites are respelled on the union value rather than with a call-site lift.

The sites where `A` may itself be a computation type, one by one:

- `Handler.scala:199, 201, 270, 272` (`A = O[X]`, abstract) and `:449, 452, 527, 530` (`Any`, erased): today `Nested.unnest` at the site, then the lift's `nest` inside the default, then `unnest` inside the two-argument apply, three type tests that net to one unnest. With one `apply` the spelling is `k(ans)` on the union value: one unnest, same payload. Writing `k(Nested.unnest(ans))` instead would summon the lift in `Handler.scala`, which the rule forbids and which is also three tests again.
- `Handler.scala:357` (`O[X0]`): the same, `k(v)`.
- `Eval.scala:645` (`Any`, erased): today the `Ensure` override delivers `v` to `use` with no unnest, so a boxed acquired value (a `Bracket` whose acquire answers a computation, `Nested(c)` once settled) reaches `use` as the box, where the ordinary path (`Arrow.scala:257`) delivers `c`. This is a latent defect on the abandonment walk, reachable only for a boxed resource. With one `apply` the site cannot type-check as written: `Any` is not `Any < S2`, and the two spellings that compile are the lift (which nests the union value a second time and delivers the box again) and the representation-assertion cast `v.asInstanceOf[Any < Any]` (which delivers the payload). The rule at `CONTRIBUTING.md:101` picks the cast; a pin is listed in section 7.
- `Batch.scala:178` (`Any < (Batch & S)`, concrete computation type) and the five test lines (section 5): today the settled overload is the only applicable one and its default nests once; with one `apply` the site fails to compile and `Kyo.lift` nests once at the site. Same payload, now visible.
- `Batch.scala:155`, `Choice.scala:99, 124`, `ArrowEffectTest.scala:2173` (`Any` or an abstract element type): `Nested.nest` at the site, one level, same as today's lift inside the default.

## 5. Compile-time findings

The `PollTest` ascriptions. `git blame` puts `PollTest.scala:321` and `:323` on `4c64b3e6fb`, whose message says: "fixes two PollTest call sites that stopped compiling under the Arrow.apply overload added in 7891153e02: with both apply(v: A) and apply(v: A < S2) candidate, the expected type no longer propagates into Present(...), so the element type is ascribed." That is the Scala rule that an overloaded method's arguments are typed without an expected type. With one `apply` the argument `Present(T.T2("zero"))` is typed against `Maybe[T.T2] < S2`; `Present[T.T2]` (`Maybe.scala:81, 93`) does not conform to the opaque type, so the lift is searched as a view, `CanLift[Present[T.T2]]` derives (`CanLift.scala:85`), and the result `Present[T.T2] < S2` conforms to `Maybe[T.T2] < S2` by the union's covariance, so the ascription should dissolve. This is exactly the compile the brief allows for in section 7, and the standing ruling of 2026-08-28 (`rulings.md:76-82`) classes the ascriptions as workarounds to be removed once the root cause is.

Other ascription workarounds: a grep of `kyo-prelude`, `kyo-core` and `kyo-kernel` tests for a cont applied to an ascribed argument finds only the two `PollTest` lines. `BracketTest.scala:228` ascribes inside `Loop.continue(1: Int < Ask)`, which is `Loop`'s argument, not a cont's.

Sites that stop compiling. A call fails only where the argument is statically a computation type and the cont's input type is that same computation type, because there the settled overload is today the only candidate (the lift refuses the type) and with one `apply` there is no candidate at all. Each becomes `cont(Kyo.lift(x))`, the spelling `CONTRIBUTING.md:335` already prescribes for an `Output` that is a computation:

- `Batch.scala:178`, `e.cont(results(e.value))`, the site `12074d8523` fixed by typing `ContCall` over the computation.
- `ArrowEffectTest.scala:337`, `t.cont(bump(t.input))`, the contract case that fix added ("typed over a computation splices the answer at the call site"); its sibling at `:345`, typed over `Any`, is the pending overload today and is unchanged.
- `ArrowEffectTest.scala:2998`, `cont(payload)` with `payload: Int < Say` on an `AskBoxed` cont.
- `ArrowEffectTest.scala:3056`, `cont(payload)` twice.
- `PendingTest.scala:803`, `cont(inner)` with `inner: Int < Ask` on a `Give` cont (`Const[Int < Ask]`, `PendingTest.scala:19`).

No site was found that relies on the settled result row `B < S` against the pending `B < (S & S2)`. For a raw argument `S2` is unconstrained by the argument, so it is instantiated from the expected type or, in a contravariant position with no bound, maximized to `Any`, giving `B < (S & Any)`, which conforms both ways to `B < S`; `Arrow.id`'s override answers `A < Any` and would answer `A < (Any & S2)`. The one shape worth a compile is an application whose result feeds a further generic call that infers its own rows from that result, `Poll.runFirst[T.T1](cont(...))` at `PollTest.scala:321` being the instance in hand: an open `S2` in the argument's row is new input to that inference.

Kernel-internal sites that would summon the lift if respelled naively, against `CONTRIBUTING.md:101`: `Eval.scala:645` (the cast spelling above), `Handler.scala:199, 201, 270, 272, 357, 449, 452, 527, 530` (`k(ans)`, `k(v)`), `ArrowEffect.scala:877, 883` (`cont(...)` on the suspension or the masked computation). None needs a summon; `Arrow.scala` loses its one summon (`:65`), which can only shorten the macro suspension cascade the kernel skill describes, and the clean batch build is still the check (section 7).

## 6. The overrides

- `Arrow.apply(f)` (`Arrow.scala:165-181`) and `recursive` (`:209-225`): the override exists so the node's own two-argument apply can call `apply(Nested.unnest(v))` and so a user can apply the arrow to a value directly. With one `apply` the two-argument apply calls `f(Nested.unnest(v))` in place, which is `map`'s shape (`Pending.scala:80`), and the anonymous class has one method fewer. Simpler, not moved. The user's direct application now takes the discriminating path; the cost is in section 4.
- `deferInline` (`Effect.scala:86-104`): the same; the settled arm becomes `cont.head(f, cont.tail)`, which is `andThen`'s arm (`Pending.scala:153`). Simpler.
- `Id` (`Arrow.scala:138-147`): `override def apply[S2](v: A < S2) = v`, a one-line override with no lift; or no override at all, since the default `this.head(v, this.tail)` is `Id.apply(v, Id)`, one `isInstanceOf[Id]` (`:142-143`). Simpler either way.
- `Ensure` (`Arrow.scala:247-258`) and its two subclasses (`Arrow.scala:196-199`, `Bracket.scala:108-120`): the abstract `apply(v: A)` is the body the final two-argument apply discriminates for, and `Eval.release` holds it erased as `Arrow.Ensure[Any, Any, Any]` (`Eval.scala:645`). The body cannot keep the name `apply`, because a raw-value `apply` on `Ensure` recreates the pair at exactly the erased site the concern is about. So the body becomes an abstract member under another name, and the shared final two-argument apply applies it to the unnested value; the kernel has no existing name for "the strict body of an arrow" (`run` is a clause's body in `Handler` and the strict local in `map`), so this is the one naming decision removal forces. The alternative, `Ensure` as a marker with each subclass writing out the three-arm discrimination, makes "applies without polling the safepoint" (`Arrow.scala:183-190`) a property of each site rather than of the class. Moved, not simplified.
- `Chain` (`Arrow.scala:265-299`): no override, provided the trait default of `apply[S2]` is `this.head(v, this.tail)`; that expression is today's settled body and it is what keeps a composed cont fused at the point of application (`Arrow.scala:58-61`). If the default stays `this(v, Arrow.id)`, `Chain` needs `override def apply[S2](v: A < S2) = a(v, b)` to hold the four rows in section 4, and the two spellings are the same thing said twice.

The surviving shape: one `apply[S2](v: A < S2): B < (S & S2)` in the trait whose body is `Debugger.onUnfused(this); this.head(v, this.tail)`, no lift anywhere in `Arrow.scala`, and every node's fast path living in its two-argument apply where the discrimination already is.
